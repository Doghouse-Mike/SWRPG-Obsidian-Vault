The ultimate example of form over function, the Max- 7 Rono from SoroSuub is one of the simplest cargo freighters on the market, with its boxy design basically unchanged for well over a century now.

Originally built for internal use by SoroSuub, the Rono consists of two sections; the first is a large hexagonal shaped hold section, the sides of which open outward and can be used as loading ramps. The second section of the ship is the elevated, front- facing command cockpit, accessible via a ladder from the hold section. In the rear of the ship are its six engines—three on each side—which include both sublight and hyperdrive units. All other extras and amenities have been stripped from the freighter. After years of internal use, the Rono was eventually launched on to the open market with the Max-3 Rono, the ship’s low cost and sturdy build ensured the ship quickly found a home with budget-conscious shipping and transport companies.

In addition to a version with standard landing gear, the Rono is also available in a track and rail configuration. In this configuration a Rono can be launched from a ramp that has a track and overhead rail. The weight has to be calibrated before launch so the adjusted thrust ratio can be calculated to allow takeoff. The weight can be determined via a load clutch handle that is next to the booster throttle, which displays the weight on a gauge. This launch system frees up open landing platform space and allows dozens of Ronos to be placed on a ramp and track and launched in quick succession.

The Rono’s lack of frills means the freighter does not have any weapons as standard, often necessitating that the ship flies with an escort. However the freighter’s armour plating and small shield is surprisingly robust and allows the Rono to withstand some sustained small laser fire. Its cockpit is designed to be simple and easy to use by hundreds of different species, though while the controls of the ship are easy to learn actually piloting the thing is a separate issue. Heavy, sluggish and slow to respond, the Rono requires a good pilot to be able to coax a strong performance out of it.
