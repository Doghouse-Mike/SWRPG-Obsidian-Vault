Part of the thrill of podracing is the near certainty of collisions. For many enthusiasts, a good race is measured by how many crashes occured, and less how fast the winner ran the circuit. Given the nature of these races, many pilots view it as a foregone conclusion that they will be involved in collisions no matter what efforts they might take to avoid them. This leads to some adopting reinforced plating for their engines, the better to withstand “accidental” impacts as they occured during a race.

More tactically-minded pilots take this even further, adding ridges, struts, and vanes designed to appear ornamental or to aid in aerodynamics, but instead intended to cushion impacts when their vehicle rams into another. That those pilots who are frequently involved in collisions and still manage to cheat death race after race garner large amount of fame (and credits) certainly has a strong bearing on other pilots’ decision to adopt such modifications and alter their pilots’ racing tactics accordingly
