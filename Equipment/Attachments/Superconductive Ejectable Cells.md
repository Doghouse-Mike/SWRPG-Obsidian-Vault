Heat management has always been a fundamental part of podracing, and many a pilot or crew have scratched their heads on ways to get around it. Between better cooling systems, more efficient engines, or creative frames, a lot of effort has been poured into heat efficiency as it directly translates into better performances. Some however push the obsession even further by using superconductive cells that can be plugged into the cooling system, and collect excess heat during engine bursts or Boosts. The cells can then be ejected off the engines when having filled their purpose.

While the detractors of the system claim that all that wasted space would be better used for additional radiators, its proponents argue that cooling heat, and preventing excess heat are not strictly the same, and just ejecting the heat directly instead of waiting for it to cool down is more efficient... at the cost of having a limited amount of uses.

Based on injectors like the similar cell based Coaxium system, this proves to be a very handy system, until the podracer runs out of charges. Once having filled its purpose, this modification becomes pretty much a dead weight.
